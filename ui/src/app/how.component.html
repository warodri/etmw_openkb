← How this works

Small reader. Shared library. A source on every answer.

Most AI loads a giant model to answer anything. OpenKB splits the job: the knowledge lives in a library people donated, and a small model on your own machine reads only the pages that matter.

From question to answer

  1. You ask. Your questions stay in your tasks. They never go into the library.
  2. The library is searched. Donated documents are converted to Markdown, split into chunks and indexed. OpenKB pulls the few passages that fit your question.
  3. The reader answers. A small open model reads only those passages and writes the answer. It names the document each fact came from.
  4. No source, no answer. If the library does not have it, the reply is "not in the KB". That refusal is enforced on the server and can't be prompted away.

Why not just a big model, or Wikipedia?

A big model memorises the world and you can't check where a fact came from. OpenKB's model reads. The library is open, versioned and owned by the people who donated to it.

Wikipedia is a fine library, and it could be one of the sources. OpenKB adds what Wikipedia doesn't carry: manuals, local knowledge, field guides and how-tos that people choose to donate, in many languages, Spanish first.

Why you can trust it

Consent first. Every donor confirms the work is theirs to share and accepts the open licence.
Reviewed before it's live. A maintainer reads each donation before it joins the library.
History and takedown. Everything is versioned. Donors can ask for removal and it really goes.
Disagreement is shown. Contested claims keep both sides and their sources instead of being deleted.

Where it's at

This is OpenKB v0.1 (Beta). In our first test on a 50-document library, the reader got 19 of 20 answerable questions right with the right source, and correctly said "not in the KB" on all 5 trick questions. That shows the loop works. It is not yet a big library, and we're not claiming it matches the large labs.
